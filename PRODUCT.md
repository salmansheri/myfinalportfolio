# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

A personal portfolio for a full-stack engineer, read by four audiences with equal weight:

- **Hiring managers and recruiters** deciding whether to interview for a full-time role.
- **Freelance clients** (founders, businesses) deciding whether to hire for a project.
- **Studios and agencies** looking for an engineer who can build and ship ambitious web work.
- **Peers and the developer community** forming an opinion of the owner's craft and reputation.

Each arrives with a different question, but all are judging the same thing: can this person be trusted to build and ship real software.

## Product Purpose

Present the owner as a full-stack engineer and turn a visit into contact: an interview, a project inquiry, or a lasting impression. Success is a visitor from any of the four audiences understanding who the owner is, what they have done, and how to reach them, without hunting.

## Positioning

**Ships end to end.** The owner takes a product from data model and backend through to a polished, interactive interface without handoffs. The site itself is part of the proof: it is built and shipped by the same person it describes.

## Operating Context

- Visitors usually arrive from a link (CV, LinkedIn, job application, proposal, social profile) and skim before deciding whether to read further.
- Recruiters often scan quickly and may revisit to check dates, roles, and contact details.
- Clients and studios evaluate whether the owner's past work matches their project.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Existing stack (keep): Next.js 16 (App Router; see AGENTS.md, this version differs from older Next.js), React 19, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS 4, Bun as package manager.
- Motion and 3D libraries already installed: GSAP with `@gsap/react`, three.js with `@react-three/fiber`.
- The repo is still the create-next-app scaffold: no real pages, copy, or metadata yet.
- Open decision: the owner's display name, location/timezone, and contact channels (email, GitHub, LinkedIn, etc.) have not been supplied. Do not guess them; ask before building contact or identity UI.

## Evidence on Hand

- **Work experience** is the primary proof: employers, roles, and dates. Details have not yet been provided and must come from the owner.
- No shipped-project case studies, writing, talks, testimonials, or client logos have been confirmed. Do not invent projects, metrics, employers, testimonials, or quotes; leave clearly marked placeholders instead.

## Product Principles

1. **The site is a shipped artifact.** It should demonstrate end-to-end competence in how it is built and how it performs, not only claim it.
2. **Every audience finds its answer fast.** Role, experience, and a way to make contact are reachable from the first screen for a skimming recruiter as much as a curious peer.
3. **Real over impressive.** Only verifiable experience and work appear; gaps stay visible as placeholders rather than filled with plausible fiction.
4. **Interaction serves credibility.** Motion and 3D exist to show craft and never block reading the experience or finding contact details.
